Two sets of European rules set requirements that a Norwegian solution for identification in education should meet.

## eIDAS 2 and the EUDI Wallet

eIDAS is the EU regulation on electronic identification and trust services. The first version came in 2014 and made it possible for national eIDs to be recognised across borders. The revised version requires the member states to offer at least one digital wallet by the end of 2026. The regulation must be incorporated into Norwegian law, and according to the draft mapping report this is planned for 2030.

The wallet holds person identification data (PID) at a high security level and attested attributes from an authentic source, for example diplomas, right to study and student status. For the education sector this means:

- **Diplomas as attestations:** the institutions, or a national source such as the National Diploma Database (NVB), can issue digital credentials directly to the wallet. The credentials must be legally valid.
- **An identifier that links the credential to the person:** in Norway probably the national identity number (fødselsnummer) or D number. Persons with a fictitious or changing number are a challenge.
- **Mobility across borders:** in the long term the wallet can supplement or take over parts of ESI, Erasmus Without Paper, EMREX and Europass, which are all issued by institutions.
- **Login from abroad:** the wallet gives foreign students and researchers a way to identify themselves to Norwegian services.

## Skills Portability Act

In September 2026 the European Commission presented a proposal for a regulation on the portability of qualifications and skills. It applies to qualifications at EQF levels 2–8, which in a Norwegian context mainly means upper secondary education, trade and journeyman's certificates, vocational college and higher education. The proposal is marked as EEA relevant.

The member states shall ensure that qualifications from the last ten years can by default be issued as digital credentials to the EUDI Wallet, and that older qualifications (10–45 years) are issued on request within two weeks. The recipient shall be able to verify that the credential is genuine, issued by an authorised issuer and not revoked. The data can be stored for up to 45 years.

## What Norway must solve

- **Linking to the right person:** the issuer must be able to link the identity in the wallet to the right person in the source register, probably the National Diploma Database, regardless of whether the person had a national identity number, D number or fictitious number when the diploma was issued.
- **History for up to 45 years:** the same person may have had several identifiers and names. A stable key with a history of linked identifiers is needed.
- **Automatic linking:** ten year groups by default and a two-week deadline for older credentials make manual case handling untenable.
- **Persons without a Norwegian ID:** former international students will request credentials with the wallet from their home country. Today, diplomas with a fictitious number do not get into the National Diploma Database or the Diploma Portal (Vitnemålsportalen).
- **All levels of education:** the regulation applies from upper secondary to higher education, so the identity should work across the levels, not only cover higher education as UH-ID does today.
- **Lookup in an authentic source:** a solution that only provides login or translation between identities, without a register and history, is not enough.
- **European standards:** W3C Verifiable Credentials, the EUDI Wallet and Europass, not a format specific to Norway.
- **Status for verification:** requirements for accreditation, suspension and revocation also apply to the source register and to those who award qualifications. For vocational college and higher education this can be solved through the education register.
- **Privacy and legal basis:** storage for 45 years, a national ID in the attestations and a life-course perspective require a clear legal basis and data minimisation.
