Who is affected by the lack of a common identification of persons in education, what happens at the different levels of education, and which needs the pilot has found. The text is based on the pilot's draft mapping report (23 October 2026).

## Who is affected

The target group spans the whole educational pathway: children in kindergarten, pupils in primary, lower secondary and upper secondary education, apprentices, students at vocational colleges, university colleges and universities, doctoral candidates, participants in further and continuing education, employees and researchers, and parents who need access to information about their own children.

Within the target group there are three groups with very different starting points:

- persons with a national identity number (fødselsnummer) or D number
- persons who stay in Norway, but are waiting for a D number or never get one
- persons who never stay in Norway, but still take part in Norwegian education or research, for example in international research projects led by Norwegian institutions

There is no overall figure for the size of the last group, partly because it is hard to count without a unique ID.

## Primary, lower secondary and upper secondary education

About 1.8 per cent of pupils in primary and lower secondary school and 1.3 per cent in upper secondary education lack a national identity number. Often the parents have received a D number in connection with work and tax liability, while it takes longer for the children to get one. In the meantime, municipalities and county authorities register fictitious numbers. The Norwegian Directorate for Education and Training (Udir) has a pattern for this in the registration handbook, but not all municipalities follow it. The work is manual and time-consuming, and often results in duplicates and poor data quality.

Pupils with a fictitious number can sit exams, but the diploma is not transferred to the National Diploma Database (NVB).

## Higher education

Each institution gives students and employees a Feide ID linked to a national identity number, D number or S number. A stay of at least six months in Norway is required to get a D number, so many exchange students never get one. In 2025 there were about 102,000 new students in higher education. 18,000 S numbers were ordered, and 8,600 of these have later received a D number. In addition, there were about 2,500 orders for employees and other groups.

When the studies are completed, the Feide ID is eventually closed. Persons with a national identity number or D number can still retrieve diplomas and results in the Diploma Portal (Vitnemålsportalen) with ID-porten. Persons with an S number cannot.

## Application and arrival

When persons without a national identity number or D number apply for admission through the Norwegian Universities and Colleges Admission Service (Samordna opptak), an S number is created. Those who are offered a place and show up get a Feide ID based on this number. If they later get a D number, the personal data are updated manually. For admission directly to an institution, for example to a master's programme, the applicant first gets a fictitious number and then an S number when the study place has been offered.

## Summary of needs

**Lack of coordination and standards**

- Municipalities and institutions create the numbers locally, without a common format or national rules.
- The numbers are locked to the individual municipality or institution and cannot follow the person when changing school owner or level of education.
- Several sectors have their own auxiliary identifiers (H number and common auxiliary number, DUF number, TRK number, S number and institution-specific numbers), which are not coordinated. It has not been examined whether they can collide with each other or with future national identity numbers and D numbers.

**No support in the National Population Register**

- The National Population Register (Folkeregisteret) has no temporary identity type between "not registered" and a full national identity number or D number.
- Norwegian eID requires registration in the National Population Register, so fictitious numbers do not allow login with ID-porten.
- The DUF number cannot be used as a school number, because it is subject to a duty of confidentiality.

**The transition to an official number**

- No automatic mechanism reliably links a fictitious number to the later D number or national identity number. Only similarity in name and address gives an indication, so the linking is done manually, with a risk of duplicates and incorrect links.
- Children and parents may lose access to decisions, allocations and history when the ID changes.
- Children often get a D number or national identity number later than their parents, so the temporary period becomes long.

**Administrative burden and data quality**

- Municipalities and institutions must create, maintain and close down fictitious persons, and spend time on manual checking and correction.
- The data flow between institutions requires a parallel paper-based process, for example for documentation of education.

**Privacy and access to information**

- Without unique identification it is harder to comply with data protection rules, for example when handling access requests, and the risk increases that information is disclosed to the wrong person.

**Registers and life-course data**

- Statistics Norway's (SSB) statistics are not fully correct, because persons with fictitious numbers cannot be followed across levels of education.
- The national education register and an extended National Diploma Database cannot link data to the right person across levels and institutions.
- Education data shall follow the person for 50 years, but foreign students and employees lose access to their own results and diplomas when their education is completed, because the institutions lack a legal basis for keeping an electronic identity.

## Concepts

The report distinguishes between identity, eID, authentication and identification. A digital service goes through several steps:

| Step | Question |
|---|---|
| Authenticate | Is the eID valid? |
| Identify | Who is the user for this service? |
| Recognise | Is this the same user as last time? |
| Link | Which role or information belongs to the user? |
| Grant access | What is the user entitled to do? |
