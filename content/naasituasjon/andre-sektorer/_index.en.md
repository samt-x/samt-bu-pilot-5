How other Norwegian sectors identify persons who are not in the National Population Register (Folkeregisteret).

## The health sector

The health sector has the most formalised Norwegian solution. A D number is the normal arrangement for patients who will be in Norway for more than six months. Where a D number is not available, an H number is used. This is an internal auxiliary number that cannot follow the patient to another organisation. That is why the **common auxiliary number** was introduced: a national, random 11-digit number that starts with 8 or 9 and can be used across organisations.

The common auxiliary number is managed in the Person Service (Persontjenesten) at Norsk helsenett. It combines the National Population Register, the Common Contact Register (Kontakt- og reservasjonsregisteret), the Guardianship Register (Vergemålsregisteret) and address data, and is limited to the health and care sector.

During the COVID-19 pandemic in 2021, a common auxiliary number was assigned automatically to arriving travellers without a national identity number (fødselsnummer) or D number. The legal basis was temporary, but the function has been continued in the Person Service. It is the closest thing there is to automatic mass registration with auxiliary numbers.

The exemption card scheme (frikort) shows the consequence of lacking a number: an automatic exemption card requires a national identity number or D number, so others must send receipts to Helfo themselves.

## The immigration administration

UDI assigns a DUF number to everyone who applies for protection or a residence permit, often long before the person gets a national identity number or D number. The number follows the case over time, and is registered in the National Population Register as a link if the person later gets a Norwegian number.

For education, the limitation is that the DUF number only covers those who apply for residence in Norway, not, for example, students on short stays. Nor is it meant as a general identifier, partly because of privacy concerns related to asylum applications.

## The labour and welfare administration

Most benefits from NAV require a national identity number or D number. Where NAV still has to register a person, for example a foreign party in a child support case, a separate personal identifier (NPID) is created. About 22,000 persons have one. NAV has routines against duplicates and can request a D number. NPID does not allow login on NAV's websites.

During the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, laid-off EEA citizens with a D number who had travelled home could not log in, because they lacked a Norwegian eID. A temporary amendment to the regulations let eID issuers use the foreign identifier in the National Population Register, so that an eID could be issued without the person appearing in person. The point for education: even with a D number, the lack of an eID can cause exclusion.

## Norwegian Customs

Persons without a national identity number or D number, or who live abroad, can apply for a TRK number (customs registered customer) in Altinn. The number works as an identifier in the customer register on a par with the national identity number and the organisation number.

## The municipal sector

Some pupils start school before they have received a D number or national identity number, often because the parents have received a D number through work while the children have not. Pupils with a fictitious number from Vigo or the exam service can sit exams, but the diploma is not transferred to the National Diploma Database (NVB).

## Transfer value

All the sectors have their own solutions, but they are made for their own purpose and are hard to transfer to the education sector.
