How the Nordic countries and the Netherlands identify persons in education, and which identifiers exist at European level.

The mapping is based on publicly available primary sources, mainly the public authorities' own documentation and legal texts, supplemented by official news items. The pilot has not been in direct contact with the authorities in the countries, so the information should be confirmed before it is used as a basis for decisions.

## Finland

Finland is the only Nordic country with its own education ID. The **learner number** (oppijanumero) is a permanent identifier for the person in the registers of the education administration. Technically it is an OID with a serial number and a check digit, not a format that resembles a personal identity number. The register is kept by the Finnish National Agency for Education (Utbildningsstyrelsen) in the service KOSKI, and the number is assigned automatically through connected services such as applications, the early childhood education register Varda and the universities' interfaces. Since 2022 it has been a general identifier in the education administration, also for teachers.

The scheme covers persons without a Finnish personal identity number, but not without problems. Without a match in the population register, a caseworker must identify the person manually. For persons without a personal identity number, a new identity is created every time the basic data are registered again, and the duplicates must be merged manually. If the person later gets a personal identity number, the two identities must also be merged manually.

## Sweden

Sweden has no separate education ID. The regulation on student registers at universities and university colleges requires name and personal identity number, or a number that is unique for the student at the institution. The requirement is thus only local uniqueness.

For persons without a personal identity number, there is a samordningsnummer (coordination number) from the Swedish Tax Agency (Skatteverket), with three identity levels from 2023, but municipalities and schools cannot request it. The common student system Ladok creates local temporary personal identity numbers, and the change to a real personal identity number is done manually. In 2023 the Swedish Higher Education Authority (Universitetskanslersämbetet) rejected a single national student register, citing privacy.

The closest Sweden has to a person-centric identity is eduID.se from Sunet, an identity service that applies before, during and after the studies, and that can be verified with a travel document for persons without a personal identity number. It is an identity service, not a register.

## Denmark

**Unilogin** is a national digital identity for pupils, parents and employees from day care through the whole of primary and lower secondary education, and it follows the child when changing school. It requires a CPR number and does not cover higher education, where the federated solution WAYF is used. Unilogin thus solves the change of school owner, but not persons without an official number.

Denmark's answer to persons without a number lies in the population register: the Danish Immigration Service (Udlændingestyrelsen) assigns **administrative CPR numbers** at the first residence permit, for example to refugees, persons reunited with family and asylum-seeking children in school. These are ordinary CPR numbers that the person keeps, so no transition from a temporary to a permanent number needs to be automated. It requires a stay of more than three months.

Substitute personal numbers (erstatningspersonnumre) are something else: they are created by the organisations themselves, without a central overview or reconciliation with a later CPR number. Foreign students often get such a number from the university and must report it themselves if they later get a CPR number.

## Iceland

Iceland has no separate education ID. Kennitala is assigned widely and quickly, also to students from countries outside the EEA as part of the residence permit. A central pupil register was taken into use in March 2026 and requires a kennitala. How pupils without a kennitala are handled is not documented in the sources.

## The Netherlands

All residents have a personal identity number (BSN). Those who do not have one get a statutory reserve number that is assigned centrally by DUO, which also owns the registers of pupils and students, one per level of education. In primary, secondary and vocational education ECK-iD is used, and in higher education SURF offers an eduID.

## European level

No European identifier provides a lifelong, person-centric education ID to build on:

- **European Student Identifier (ESI)** is the hub of Erasmus Without Paper and the European Student Card. It is meant to be permanent and globally unique, but is issued and managed by the institution.
- **The EUDI Wallet** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1183) shall be offered in all member states by the end of 2026, and holds both person identification and attested attributes, such as diplomas. The large-scale pilot DC4EU, in which Norway took part, tested digital diplomas.
- **European Learning Model and Europass Digital Credentials** are based on W3C Verifiable Credentials.
- **EMREX and ELMO** form a decentralised network for exchanging results across borders. The Diploma Portal (Vitnemålsportalen) is the Norwegian contact point.

No European framework requires or recommends a national lifelong ID for learners. What the new European rules require of a Norwegian solution is described under [Future requirements](/en/pilotering/pilot-5/loesningsvalg/framtidige-krav/).
